## Open questions

From names-suffix (2026-11-18):
- **IDs that differ only in case share a room-name domain** (`Gabriel-LAN` and `gabriel-lan` are two rooms, both
  `gabriel-lan.p2p.internal`): the brief said to lower-case; leaving such rooms the tag alone would need the ID to be
  all lower case (stricter), the user's call. Likewise "looks derived" is read strictly (any 8 characters of base32's,
  and a private room's form, both left the tag alone); a narrower reading is the user's call too.

From N6b (2026-11-18):
- **The long run's relay traffic is past "a few hundred requests a service"**: a room's own cadence (a server alone
  asks "who serves?" about every 5 minutes, three events each; the agent's publisher connects hourly; a member's names
  client reconnects at a lookup after 10 idle minutes) sends each relay ~40 events and ~6 connections an hour, ~1,000
  events a day (the proof run). The script's caps (2,000 events and 300 connections a relay a day, past which it
  ends) are a safety valve at that rate, not the kickoff's few hundred; a shorter run (`timeout 1d`, ~1,000 a relay)
  or fewer relays in the link (`--nostr`) is the user's call. The calendars and explorers stay far under theirs (150
  each over the run; the proof: 5 or 6 a calendar, 4 at blockstream.info).
- **A relay that doesn't answer is retried every few seconds for good**: relay.degmods.com "didn't answer" ~120 times
  in the proof's 1.8 hours, each a close and a new connection 2 to 16 s on (142 connections; the backoff restarts
  after each connection that opens). A longer backoff for a relay that keeps not answering is rooms.md's, not this
  track's (docs/backlog.md).
- **Two partners rarely sync into a new server at once** (a server's question stops at the first round with an
  answer and keeps what answered): a first sync is mostly one partner's rate. Asking on to the last round, as a
  member does with one answer, would double a new server's rate where two serve, at a question's cost; not done.

From N6a (2026-11-18):
- **The bound drops the youngest chains first** (was the longest unasked: junk registered in bulk dropped only the
  room's names). Built within the decisions; what it gives up, for the user to know: an honest room past the bound
  drops its newest names, and junk claimed early and kept renewed still pushes out names claimed after it.
- **"Nobody serves" under load**: a member that never heard the room takes silence through every round for nobody
  serving (NXDOMAIN 30 s); 30 of 500 members joining in a minute did. A first silence as SERVFAIL (a room with no
  server then answers NXDOMAIN ~2.5 s later than N3's measured 2.6 s), or reply budgets that grow with the room
  (more amplification for forged asks: 600 replies/s at 300 servers now)?
- **Forged asks at hundreds of servers**: thinning `dissent` replies by the room's size would cost the guarantee that
  one honest holder is enough.

From N0c (2026-11-18):
- **A measured GPU figure needs the user's OK to install a tool on the Mac host** (hashcat, or a WebGPU Argon2
  kernel in Chrome for Testing): nothing that runs Argon2 on a GPU is installed; N0c's figure is published Chukwa v2
  rates (Argon2id 1 MiB, t = 4) scaled to t = 1 and by bandwidth, which agree with N0a's bandwidth bound.

From review 3's fix loop (2026-11-18), each fixed as conservatively as the decisions allow; the user's call:
- **A contest crowded by forged repliers** (finding 2): copies of a key's real entry from made-up servers, claiming
  `since` 0, crowd out its real holder; the contest now ends within 10 s with no answer, never another owner's. So
  anyone who knows the room's ID can keep a *contested* name (one with a second live chain: a registration) from
  resolving on first lookups. Closing it needs a reply to prove its server (a serve-style proof over the nonce): a
  protocol change, and the replier's identity in every reply.
- **The spread under forged replies** (finding 2): forged nones and copies of real entries still count toward the
  next first lookup's spread (narrower by 3 bits, to 15 at most); its rounds widen to everyone, so a lookup is slower
  by about a second, not wrong.
- **A refresh that can't see the kept owner's claim** (finding 1): while a member never saw the kept chain's claim, a
  refresh whose two drawn servers show only another key's run keeps what is kept (refused). A first lookup's `ask`
  would find the owner's holders but announces the name id from a member that keeps it (decision 12 as widened covers
  only names not kept); not done.
